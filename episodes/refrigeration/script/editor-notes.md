## Cleveland Clinic — Recommendation

**Finding:** The May 15, 1929 Cleveland Clinic disaster (123 dead from burning X-ray film releasing phosgene/methyl chloride; the third-floor clock stopped at 11:30) appears in **neither** ch03 nor ch04, despite ch04's front matter claiming it was deferred to ch04. It is simply absent.

**Assessment:** Per the blueprint, this beat is explicitly **optional/brief** (blueprint W3 "writer note, ~2 min" but also flagged in Open Questions #4 and the W2 length-vs-density tension as a cut candidate). W3/ch04 is already dense and tonally heavy — it carries the Berlin family deaths, Midgley's tragic arc, the ozone hole, DuPont, Montreal, Kigali, the Road Not Taken, the cooling gap, and the AI exchange. The toxic-refrigerant body count is **already established** in ch03 (lines 291–295: methyl chloride odorless, families dying in their sleep, the 1926 Berlin family) and re-stated in ch04 (lines 31–38). The narrative function the Cleveland Clinic was meant to serve — "1929 America was acutely afraid of refrigerant/gas deaths, which primed the market for Midgley's safe Freon" — is already carried by the Berlin-family beat and the in-the-papers framing.

**Recommendation: accept the omission. Do not add it.** Adding a 123-death hospital-gas disaster to ch04 would (a) push an already-overloaded chapter longer, (b) stack a second mass-casualty bodily-horror beat on top of the Berlin family within minutes, risking the PG-13/gravity balance the blueprint specifically worried about (Open Questions #4), and (c) duplicate the "refrigerant gas kills people → market wants Freon" point that's already made. If the team feels the *market-priming* mechanism needs to be explicit, the lighter fix is a **single sober sentence** in ch04 around line 99 (where Freon adoption is described as "within five years every major American manufacturer is running Freon") — e.g., Jeff noting "and the public was primed for it; gas-leak deaths were front-page news that year" — without naming the Cleveland body count. But my recommendation is to leave it out entirely; the chapter is stronger and cleaner without it, and nothing load-bearing is lost. **Action item for the team:** correct the ch04 front-matter line that claims Cleveland Clinic was "held for chapter 4," since it was actually dropped — the note is now misleading.
