## Unverifiable Claims

**1. The Cold-Storage Banquet capon killed on Valentine's Day (Ch. 04, line 136)**

The script says: "a capon that had reportedly been killed on Valentine's Day." Research flags this as unverified — sourced to Nicola Twilley's *Frostbite* but not independently confirmed in accessible sources. However, the Atlas Obscura article (based on *Frostbite*) quotes the association's vice president directly addressing the crowd: "Your capon received its summons to the great unknown along about last St. Valentine's day." This is specific enough to be genuine and is consistent with the October 23 banquet date (approximately nine months after Valentine's Day). The script's "reportedly" hedge is appropriate and should be retained.

**Verdict: Likely Correct. The "reportedly" hedge in the script is appropriate. No change needed.**

**2. The Berlin family killed by refrigerant fumes (Ch. 03-04)**

Research notes (correctly) that no primary source identifies the family by name, address, or exact date. The Einstein-Szilard patent record and multiple credible secondary sources (APS, Smithsonian Lemelson Center, Wikipedia) confirm the general story — that Einstein was motivated by a newspaper account of a family killed by refrigerant fumes. The script does not over-specify and simply says "a Berlin family." This is appropriately hedged.

**Verdict: Likely Correct. Appropriately hedged in script. No change needed.**

**3. Tudor's exact net worth at death — "roughly two hundred million dollars in today's money" (Ch. 01-02)**

Multiple secondary sources cite this figure, but none document the inflation-adjustment methodology. The research itself flags this gap. Tudor's actual wealth at death (1864) was approximately $200,000, and the inflation to 2024 dollars depends on which deflator is used. The script's "roughly" and "equivalent" hedges are appropriate.

**Verdict: Likely Correct. Appropriately hedged. No change needed.**

## Disputed Claims

**1. Midgley death circumstances — accident vs. suicide (Ch. 04)**

The script originally framed this as a pure accident ("becomes entangled"). The coroner's official ruling was suicide. Most popular histories describe it as accidental, including Wikipedia and most secondary sources. Historians who have studied Midgley (notably Bill Kovarik) note the coroner's ruling and cite possible psychosis from lead exposure as a potential factor. The true circumstances are genuinely uncertain.

**Resolution: Script corrected** to acknowledge the dispute and honor the ambiguity. See Corrections Made section.

**2. Global cooling demand projection — "33-fold by 2100" (Ch. 05)**

Sources disagree significantly on the scale of this projection. The IEA's established figure is a tripling by 2050. An IPCC-cited figure (via Christian Science Monitor, 2014) references a 30-fold increase in AC energy use by 2100 — a closer match, but for air conditioning specifically and under high-warming scenarios. The 33-fold figure appears to derive from regional projections (e.g., Brazil-specific models) rather than a single global consensus number.

**Resolution: Script corrected** in both instances to use hedged framing ("some projections," "more than thirty-fold") while also adding context that the IEA projects tripling by 2050. The underlying concern is real and well-documented; only the precision of the single figure was overstated.
