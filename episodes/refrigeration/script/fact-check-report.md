## How It Works (ch03) — physics check

Checked line by line against NIST saturation data (research cache) and standard thermodynamics. **Physically correct; no changes required.**

- **Frame.** "You don't make cold. You move heat… the first law… the second law says heat won't flow uphill… A refrigerator is a machine that pays, with a motor, to push heat from a cold box into a warm kitchen." Correct.
- **Fluid cycles liquid ↔ gas.** Stated explicitly ("there is a liquid in there… it keeps changing back and forth between liquid and gas"). Correct.
- **Boiling points.** Ammonia "about minus thirty-three Celsius — twenty-seven below zero Fahrenheit" (NIST −33.3°C = −27.9°F ✓). Isobutane "around minus twelve Celsius" (NIST −11.7°C ✓). Isobutane "at kitchen pressure boils at twelve below" ✓.
- **Latent heat.** "Boiling that kettle completely dry takes five or six times as much" as bringing it to a boil (2,257 kJ/kg vs 335–418 kJ/kg → 5.4–6.7× ✓). "Ammonia carries about six times what the later fluids carry" (1,344 vs 216 kJ/kg for R-134a = 6.2× ✓; vs isobutane 3.6× — "the later fluids" is read as the CFC/HFC family, which is fair).
- **Evaporator.** Low-pressure liquid boils inside the box at about −25°C, drawing heat from the food. Correct. The evaporating temperature is stated as "twenty-five below zero" without a unit after two Celsius figures; −25°C (−13°F) or −25°F (−32°C) are both within the range of real household evaporators, so no risk either way.
- **Compressor.** "Welded steel dome… electric motor bolted to a small piston in a cylinder… about thirty-five hundred strokes a minute" (Secop: 3,500 rpm at 60 Hz ✓, hermetic shell ✓). "Squeezing a gas heats it" ✓. Raises pressure *and* temperature ✓.
- **Condensing temperature.** "Squeeze it to about six atmospheres and it condenses at forty-five Celsius — a hundred and thirteen Fahrenheit" (NIST: 6.0 bar ≈ 5.9 atm at 45°C; 45°C = 113°F ✓). "Now, relative to that gas, your kitchen is the cold thing" ✓.
- **Condenser.** Outside coil sheds heat; gas condenses to a warm liquid; "Condensing releases exactly the heat that boiling absorbed" ✓ (per unit mass, at the respective states — correct to first order).
- **Expansion.** Capillary tube; "pressure collapses by about ten to one" (NIST 10.3:1 for isobutane ✓); some liquid flashes; temperature drops to the new boiling point ✓; enters the evaporator as a cold liquid-vapor mixture ✓. "Low pressure, cold, inside. High pressure, hot, outside" ✓.
- **Energy accounting.** "Heat out equals heat in plus the work you paid for" — first law ✓. "The box moves more heat than the electricity it eats. Well above one to one" — COP > 1 ✓ (research: Carnot limit 3.5 at −25/45°C; real boxes lower; the script gives no number, as recommended).
- **Consequences.** "An air conditioner is the same machine with the condenser coil hung outside" ✓. Sponge analogy stated and corrected ("Squeezing doesn't push the heat out… it makes the heat flow in the right direction") ✓ — the correction the research's analogy table asked for.
- **Handoff.** Five requirements for a refrigerant; "Ammonia fails 'not kill you.' Ether… fails 'not burn.'" ✓. Linde's shaft seal as the failure point; GE's hermetic dome as the fix ✓ (de.wikipedia; Wikipedia *Christian Steenstrup*).
