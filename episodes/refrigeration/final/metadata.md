# Episode Metadata: Refrigeration

---

## Episode Title Options

1. **Refrigeration: Cold, Built In** *(working title — clean but could be stronger)*
2. **The Ice Fool: How a Laughingstock Built the World's Dependency on Cold** *(too long; undersells the full arc)*
3. **Cold Blood: The Two Centuries That Made Us Dependent on Something We Can't See** *(evocative but potentially confusing)*
4. **How Cold Built Everything** *(simple, confident, accurate)*
5. **Refrigeration: The Technology That Manufactured Human Desire — and Then the Ozone Hole** *(honest but unwieldy)*

**Top choice: "Refrigeration: How Cold Built Everything"**

Rationale: "How Cold Built Everything" is a genuine claim — politics, food systems, public health infrastructure, the modern city — and it's surprising enough to make someone stop and think. It's also slightly counterintuitive phrasing ("cold built"?) that earns a second look. Avoids the overused "Changed/Transformed the World" pattern. The thesis of the episode — that manufacturing desire for cold was the foundational act — lives inside the word "built."

## Episode Description

*(For podcast apps — 2-3 sentences; should hook, not summarize)*

In 1806, a twenty-three-year-old Bostonian loaded a ship with pond ice and sailed it to the Caribbean. Everyone laughed. Two centuries later, thirty percent of all electricity on Earth powers the thing he started. This is the story of refrigeration — three waves of breakthrough and resistance, the men who got destroyed building it, and the molecule that made it universal and quietly punched a hole in the ozone layer.
